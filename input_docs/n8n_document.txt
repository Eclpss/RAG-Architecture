The Business Scenario Is a Uniform Description of Value
Building on the need for the "Why", the Business Scenario acts as a precise, standardized definition of value.
The Definition
A mechanism to validate the premise behind an architecture effort. It must capture:
☑ Real business problems
☑ The business and technology environment
☑ Value streams enabled by capabilities
☑ Desired outcomes
Distinctions
NOT a Use Case
Use cases describe detailed software interactions.
NOT a Business Case
Business cases are for
NOT on
The Core Standard:
A Business Scenario must be SMART (Specific, Measurable, Actionable, Realistic, Time-bound).


The Anatomy of a Business Scenario
1. PROBLEM
Pain points, barriers, issues.
2. ENVIRONMENT
Business & Tech context, Value Streams.
3. OUTCOMES
The Desired Result (Must be SMART).
4. ACTORS
Human & Computer Actors.
The Problem exists in the Environment, necessitating Outcomes, which are achieved by Human and Computer Actors.

Navigating the Three Phases of Maturity
Phase 1: Premise Formulation
Input: An unstated question or vague directive (e.g., "We need an ERP").
Action: Identify the "Solution looking for a problem".
Output: List of key questions & stakeholders.
Phase 2: Initial Verification
Action: Face-to-face stakeholder engagement.
Goal: Confirm the problem is real.
Output: Draft Business Scenario (Verified but incomplete).
Phase 3: Definition
Action: Iterative analysis
Goal: Make it Fit-for-Purpose.
Output: Approved Business Scenario ready for Architecture Vision.
